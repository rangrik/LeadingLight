# Capture while reading

Record and Photo sit in the page header. Keep your book open and capture a thought
in the document or notebook you are reading.

## Speak

Press the microphone (**Quick record**) or **⇧⌘R** once. Press **Stop** or the same shortcut to finish.
Audio is saved first; the transcript follows as editable paragraphs. The native Mac,
iPad and iPhone apps use Apple's on-device speech recognition on OS 26 or newer.
The first use may download a language model. Unsupported devices or languages keep
the audio and offer **Retry transcription**. Browser builds save audio without
transcribing. The shortcut works while LeadingLight is in front.
Press Stop before switching pages to keep the take; leaving during recording cancels it.

## Write by hand

Choose **Write on this page** in a document's header to draw directly over its
text or images. The page stays open with its normal type, properties and sidenotes.
The writing controls take the place of capture controls in the same header.
Choose Pen or Eraser in **Writing tools**; **Undo** reverses the last page edit,
including a stroke. **Redo** is in Writing tools.
**Finish writing** returns to normal text editing and leaves all ink visible.

Use **Write below** on a block, or **Writing tools → Write below selected block**,
to make handwriting space between typed blocks. Writing near its bottom extends
the space and moves following blocks down. **Continue typing** adds a normal
paragraph after it. On iPad, Pencil draws and fingers scroll; **Draw with finger**
enables finger drawing explicitly. On Mac, drag with the mouse.
Document handwriting currently uses normal page zoom. Pinching out of that zoom
finishes the stroke; return to normal zoom before writing again.

Ink moves, copies and undoes with its block. Unchanged marked words can move with
their annotation. If you change those words or they wrap into a different line
shape, the original ink and context stay available for review rather than appearing
over different words. Restoring the original words and layout restores alignment.
Page history includes document handwriting. Existing notes and notebook files are
not converted when you enable writing.
An annotated list's block controls select the whole list so its ink travels with it.
Use the updated app on each device that edits these pages. An older version may
not understand the new handwriting blocks.

## Standalone notebooks

Choose **New notebook** beside New page, or in the Notebooks list. A notebook is a
separate kind of page with full-page paper. It cannot be converted to a document,
and changing a document's type cannot turn it into a notebook.

To keep separate handwritten notes about a document, choose **Page actions →
New linked notebook**. Your document, blocks, sidenotes and metadata stay intact.
The notebook's **Notes on** property links back to it. The same action subsequently
reads **Open linked notebook**. The document also lists its related notebooks.
Deleting the source document does not delete the notebook.

On iPad and iPhone, tap the paper to open handwriting, or choose **Notebook options
→ Open handwriting**. A single top row holds drawing, Text, undo/redo, Record,
Photo and options. On iPhone, the less-used tools are in options. Tap the active
pen for its width; **All pens…** opens PencilKit's palette. Choose Plain, Ruled or
Dotted paper in options. Pinch to zoom and scroll; **Add paper** extends the sheet.
iPad drawing uses Apple Pencil; iPhone drawing accepts a finger.

Choose **Text**, then tap with a finger to place one typed note between handwritten
passages. The keyboard stays closed until that tap. **Finish typing**, **Escape**
or **⌘Enter** ends the note and returns to Pen. Dismissing the keyboard also ends
Text mode. Pencil touches stay with drawing. Double tap follows your Apple Pencil
setting: eraser, previous tool, palette, or Off.

Choose **Text** to work with existing notes. Tap one to select it, then again to edit. Drag it to move it; drag its
right-edge dot to resize. Hold it for **Move…** or **Delete**. Ink and text save as
you work; Back returns to the notebook page. A local draft recovers interrupted saves.

On Mac, **Text box** places a typed note; double-click a note or select it and press
Enter to edit. Drag its body to move it and its dot to resize it. Delete removes
the selected item; its small options menu also has Delete. Mac paper edits use
page history; keyboard Undo is not connected to paper items yet. Ink is view-only
on Mac. **Notebook options** contains Zoom, Paper style, More paper and history.
A phone web preview keeps **Place text box** in that menu.

**Notebook details** opens title, description, tags and properties in a sheet without
moving the paper. On iPhone, find it in **Notebook options**. Edit or clear **Notes on** there; it offers documents rather than the notebook itself. The Notebook type stays fixed.
Old pages that already contain both blocks and paper show their original document
and metadata above the handwriting. Opening them does not migrate or discard data.
History previews paper and placed text before you restore a version. Keeping a history
copy leaves the source’s quick action pointing to its oldest linked notebook. If this
app version cannot read a notebook’s paper, it preserves the data, explains the problem
and offers history without turning the notebook into a document.

Ink, text positions and attachments travel through encrypted sync. Old handwriting
image blocks still offer **Edit handwriting**.

Record changes to a stop button and timer without moving the paper. The original
audio stays as a playable source, and its transcript becomes a separate text box.
Recognized recordings and photos sit as small source marks beside their text.
Moving the text moves its source mark; **Open original photo** shows the full image.
On Mac, right-click the photo for its menu; on iPad, hold it in Text mode. Deleting
a source keeps its extracted text. Native notebooks can record the next thought while an earlier take transcribes.
Hold a recording in Text mode to retry transcription. Photo saves the original
image before reading its text; hold it for **Extract text** to retry. If you press
Back during recognition, the source stays saved for later retry.

## Photograph a passage

Tap **Photo** on iPad or iPhone. After the first camera permission prompt, this opens
the rear camera directly. Accept the picture to save it in the page. Text recognition
then adds recognised words underneath. The original picture stays even if OCR finds
nothing. Crooked pages, small print and handwriting can need corrections.

Select any saved picture or handwriting preview and choose **Extract text** to run
Apple Vision on the device. Review its words in the page. Undo removes the inserted
words without removing the picture. The browser build cannot run Apple's recognition.

## Read PDF and EPUB books

Choose **Page actions → Import a book** (or **Notebook options → Import a book**), then **Import PDF or EPUB**.
Imports accept files up to 32 MiB so the original can sync to every device. Enter a
PDF password first if the file needs one.

A Book page holds the original and links to reading sections. PDFs keep page numbers
and split every ten pages; EPUBs follow chapter order. These are ordinary editor
pages: select words to highlight or add a sidenote. **Contents**, **Previous section**
and **Next section** move through the book. **Open original** opens its exact layout.
Text extraction reflows the PDF and may need correction, especially for columns and
complex layouts. Pages without selectable text become images; use **Extract text**
on those in an Apple app. Imported EPUBs do not run scripts or load remote images.

## Kindle purchases and Libby loans

**Kindle notes** and **Libby notes** create a Book page with its reader link and any
highlights you paste. On Mac, **Read in Kindle/Libby** opens the authorized website in
LeadingLight's browser beside your notes. On iPad/iPhone it opens the service in the
system browser. Sign in to the service yourself. Availability depends on its reader.

Protected book text stays in Kindle or Libby; it is not imported or synchronized by
LeadingLight. The reading document holds highlights, sidenotes and inline handwriting;
a separate linked notebook remains available for full-page paper.
For eligible Kindle purchases, Amazon's **Manage Your Content and Devices** offers
EPUB/PDF downloads that can be imported. For SF Public Library loans, use your library
card in Libby. Export notes through **Manage Loan → Reading Journey → Actions →
Export Reading Data**, then paste them into your notebook. Exported highlights remain
yours to reference after a loan is returned.
