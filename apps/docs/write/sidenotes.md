# Sidenotes, thoughts and essays

A sidenote is a thought you write in the margin, beside the sentence or paragraph it is about. Every sidenote on a page is also collected on that page's **Thoughts** page, so you can read all your raw thoughts in one place. When you are ready, you can start an essay beside the page and quote the source into it.

## Add a sidenote

1. Select a sentence, or any words.
2. Click **Add sidenote** in the toolbar, or press <kbd>⇧⌘M</kbd>.

The words get a dotted underline and a small number after them. A note opens in the margin, level with the words, with the cursor in it. Type your thought. It is saved like the rest of the page.

To note a whole paragraph, put the cursor in it without selecting anything and press <kbd>⇧⌘M</kbd>. In an empty paragraph, nothing happens.

To make room for the margin, the sidebar collapses and the text moves left.

## Read your notes in the margin

- Notes are numbered in reading order, from the top of the page, whatever order you added them in.
- Each note sits level with its words, or just below the note above it. Notes never cover each other.
- Rest the pointer on a note or its number, and a line runs from the words to the note.

## Use the Thoughts page

The first sidenote on a page makes a **Thoughts** page, shown nested under the page in the sidebar.

Open **Thoughts** to see every note, each under the words it belongs to. Click the quoted words to jump back: the page opens with those words in view, briefly lit. Edits you make to a note on the Thoughts page show in the margin too.

## Remove a sidenote

- Point at the note and click **Remove sidenote**. The words lose their underline and number, and the note leaves both the margin and the Thoughts page.
- If you delete the noted words instead, the note leaves the margin but stays on the Thoughts page.
- If you delete a thought on the Thoughts page, its words are no longer noted and the note leaves the margin.
- If you delete the Thoughts page, every note leaves the page at once. Your next sidenote makes a new Thoughts page.

## Start an essay

1. Open **Page options** on the page.
2. Choose **Start an essay on this page**.

An essay page opens on the right with the cursor in its title. The source page stays on the left, with its notes in the margin. The essay is nested under the page in the sidebar, and the sidebar collapses.

To close the essay, click **Close page** in its pane. To open it again, choose **Open essay** in **Page options**, or click the essay in the sidebar; the page opens with its essay beside it.

### When the window is narrow

The margin always keeps its room. If the window is too narrow for both pages and the margin, the page you last worked in stays open and the other folds into a slim strip with its title. Click the strip to bring that page back; the other one becomes the strip.

## Quote into the essay

Once a page has an essay, the selection toolbar on the page offers **Add to essay**.

1. Put the cursor in the essay where the quote should go.
2. Select a sentence in the source page.
3. Click **Add to essay**.

The essay gets a quote of the sentence after the paragraph where its cursor was, ending in a reference to the source page. The sentence in the source gets a quote mark after it.

- Click the reference in the essay to scroll the source to the sentence, briefly lit.
- Click the quote mark in the source to scroll the essay to the quote.

A quote and its sentence stay linked. Delete the quote, or only its reference, and the sentence loses its underline and quote mark at once; the other quoted sentences keep theirs. Undo in the essay brings both back. Delete the essay page and no sentence keeps a quote mark, and **Page options** offers **Start an essay on this page** again.

## Thoughts and essays in the sidebar

A Thoughts page and an essay are not listed on their own at the top of the sidebar. They show under their page on a small tree when that page is open, with the branch to the one you are on drawn in colour. Open another page and the tree folds away.

Their row menu has **Copy link** and **Delete**. If you delete the page they belong to, its Thoughts page and essay stay, listed at the top level.

::: info Limits
Sidenotes need words to sit on, so images, video, files, embeds, code blocks and dividers can't have one. You can't add sidenotes inside an essay or a Thoughts page, or quote into the essay from a note. A reference keeps the source's title from when you quoted it, and does nothing if the source was deleted.
:::
